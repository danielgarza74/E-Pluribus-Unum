PART 3SECTION CHECKS · 1.1–1.4

Section Checkpoints

Choose the best answer. A short reason appears when you get one right.

12.1 Why did the alliance system turn the Sarajevo assassination into a continental war?
Alliances were meant to keep a balance of power, but they ensured that a local conflict quickly escalated as each ally was pulled in.
12.1 How did American loans to the warring nations help make strict neutrality “impossible”?
By 1917 the United States had lent over $2 billion to the Allies and only about $27 million to Germany, so an Allied loss could mean unpaid debts.
12.1 Why did Wilson react more sharply to German U-boats than to the British blockade?
The text explains that British seizures cost property, which could be compensated after the war, while German attacks cost lives.
12.1 How did the Zimmermann Note change American opinion in the West and Midwest?
The note offered Mexico lost territory in Texas, New Mexico, and Arizona, turning a distant maritime dispute into a local, personal threat.
12.2 What was the main job of the War Industries Board under Bernard Baruch?
The WIB coordinated war production and standardized goods, while industrial output rose about 20 percent and consumer prices nearly doubled.
12.2 How did Herbert Hoover’s Food Administration differ from the War Industries Board?
Hoover urged “Meatless Tuesdays,” “Wheatless Wednesdays,” and Victory Gardens, and food shipments to the Allies tripled.
12.2 What conclusion did Justice Holmes reach in Schenck v. United States?
Holmes wrote for a unanimous Court that free speech is not absolute and that the Espionage Act conviction stood.
12.2 Which pair of factors best explains why the Great Migration accelerated between 1916 and 1919?
Push factors included Jim Crow, racial violence, and a boll weevil infestation; pull factors were factory jobs and labor shortages as men went to war.
12.3 How did the convoy system help the United States move an army to Europe?
Of the two million soldiers who crossed, not one troopship was sunk by a U-boat.
12.3 Why did Pershing refuse the “amalgamation” plan?
Pershing argued that a separate AEF was essential for American prestige and for a “seat at the table” after the war.
12.3 Why did the Western Front become a stalemate of trenches?
Machine guns could fire up to 600 rounds per minute, which the text calls the primary reason for the murderous stalemate.
12.4 What was the “heart” of Wilson’s Fourteen Points, according to the text?
The text says the sixth through thirteenth points, centered on self-determination, were the heart of Wilson’s plan.
12.4 Why did Clemenceau and Lloyd George resist Wilson’s idealism at Paris?
France had lost 1.4 million soldiers, and Lloyd George faced public pressure to make Germany pay.
12.4 What combination of events led the Senate to reject the treaty in 1919 and 1920?
The final vote on March 19, 1920 won 49 votes, seven short of two-thirds.
