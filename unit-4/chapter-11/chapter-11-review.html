PART 3SECTION CHECKS · 1.1–1.4

Section Checkpoints

Choose the best answer. A short reason appears when you get one right.

11.1 How did the Bayonet Constitution of 1887 change who held power in Hawaii?
Armed planters forced Kalakaua to sign. The property and income rules for voting disqualified most native Hawaiians, and the king lost most of his power.
11.1 Why did Hawaiian planters push for annexation by the United States after the McKinley Tariff of 1890?
The tariff ended Hawaii’s duty-free advantage. As part of the United States, its sugar would be “domestic,” and planters would earn a government bounty.
11.2 According to Mahan, what three things did the United States need to become a world power?
Mahan said the sea decided national greatness. Steamships needed coal, so a navy and a merchant fleet were useless without overseas bases.
11.2 How did overproduction and the Panic of 1893 push American leaders toward overseas expansion?
With unsold goods piling up and unemployment near 19 percent, many business leaders and politicians decided foreign markets, such as China, were essential.
11.2 What role did Social Darwinism and the “White Man’s Burden” play in the push for empire?
Josiah Strong and Kipling made empire sound like a noble mission. The text calls this a “moral cloak” over motives of profit and power.
11.3 How did the rivalry between Hearst’s Journal and Pulitzer’s World affect the road to war with Spain?
To win readers, both papers ran sensational stories. The text says the press became “an active participant in foreign policy.”
11.3 The press blamed Spain for the explosion of the USS Maine. What did later investigations suggest?
A 1976 Navy study led by Admiral Rickover concluded that an internal fire was the likely cause, yet in 1898 emotion mattered more than facts.
11.3 Why did the Anti-Imperialist League oppose taking the Philippines in the Treaty of Paris?
League members such as Carnegie, Gompers, William James, and Mark Twain argued that governing without consent would destroy democracy from within.
11.4 Why did fighting break out between American and Filipino forces in 1899?
Aguinaldo had declared independence in 1898 and felt betrayed when he was left out of the Paris talks and the islands were purchased for $20 million.
11.4 What did the Insular Cases decide about territories such as the Philippines and Puerto Rico?
The Court split territories into “incorporated” and “unincorporated.” Only fundamental rights were guaranteed in the second group, creating a two-tiered system.
11.4 What was the main goal of John Hay’s Open Door notes of 1899 and 1900?
Hay wanted the economic benefits of empire without ruling territory. After the Boxer Rebellion he also pledged to protect China’s territorial and administrative entity.
11.5 How did the United States gain the Canal Zone after Colombia’s Senate rejected a treaty?
Panama declared independence on November 3, 1903, and the United States recognized it. Panama’s treaty then granted the ten-mile-wide Canal Zone.
11.5 How did the Roosevelt Corollary change the meaning of the Monroe Doctrine?
Where the original doctrine said “hands off” to Europe, the Corollary said “hands on”: the United States might exercise an “international police power.”
11.5 Which fact best shows a contradiction in Wilson’s “moral diplomacy”?
Wilson intervened in the hemisphere more often than Roosevelt or Taft: Haiti in 1915, the Dominican Republic in 1916, and Veracruz in 1914.
