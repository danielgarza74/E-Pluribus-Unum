PART 3SECTION CHECKS · 1.1–1.4

Section Checkpoints

Choose the best answer. A short reason appears when you get one right.

7.1 Why did Southern Democrats in the 1890s decide to eliminate the Black vote entirely?
The Populists tried to unite poor white and Black farmers against the planter elite. Democrats feared this alliance, so they chose to remove Black voters.
7.1 In the Civil Rights Cases of 1883, why did the Court say the Civil Rights Act of 1875 was unconstitutional?
The Court held that the 14th Amendment applied only to state actions, so a private hotel owner could hang a “Whites Only” sign without violating the Constitution.
7.1 Look at Figure 7.1. Why did the Comité des Citoyens choose Homer Plessy to challenge the law?
Plessy bought a first-class ticket and then told the conductor he was Black, which led to his planned arrest and set up the test case.
7.1 Louisiana’s Black registered voters fell from 130,334 in 1896 to 1,342 in 1904. What does this data suggest about the new voting rules?
A drop of over 95 percent in four years shows the “color-blind” poll tax, literacy test, and Grandfather Clause worked as a targeted attack on Black citizenship.
7.2 Why did sharecroppers so often end the year in debt to the planter?
Sharecroppers started each season with no cash, so they bought food and tools on credit at the planter’s store, where interest rates often reached 50 or 60 percent.
7.2 How did vagrancy laws help supply workers to the convict lease system?
Being unemployed or “idling” was a crime. A man who could not pay the fine ended up in the penal system and was leased out to a company.
7.2 Look at Figure 7.6. Why did the text say convict leasing could be even worse for workers than slavery?
An enslaver had money invested in a person’s life. A company leasing a convict could simply replace one who died, and camp mortality reached 30 to 40 percent in the worst years.
7.2 What did Ida B. Wells’s study of Southern newspapers show about the victims of lynching?
Wells used the white press’s own reports to show that most victims were not accused of rape, and that jealousy of Black business success and land often drove the violence.
7.3 What did Booker T. Washington mean by saying races could be “as separate as the fingers, yet one as the hand”?
In his Atlanta speech Washington reassured white Southerners that Black progress would not threaten the social order, which critics later called the Atlanta Compromise.
7.3 In The Souls of Black Folk, what three things did Du Bois say Washington asked Black people to give up?
Du Bois argued that without the vote, civil rights, and higher education, Black Americans could not protect their property or train the leaders they needed.
7.3 How was the NAACP different from the earlier Niagara Movement?
The NAACP combined Du Bois’s radical ideas with the money and connections of white progressives, and its first major win was Guinn v. United States in 1915.
7.3 Look at Figure 7.12. Which of these was a pull factor in the Great Migration?
World War I stopped European immigration, so Northern factories sent labor agents south with train fare and higher wages. The other three options are push factors.
