PART 3SECTION CHECKS · 1.1–1.4

Section Checkpoints

Choose the best answer. A short reason appears when you get one right.

6.1 Why did the federal government give the Union Pacific and Central Pacific loans and alternating sections of land?
The line crossed thousands of miles, and no single company had enough capital, so Congress shared the risk with land and loans.
6.1 Figure 6.2 compares the Homestead dream with the Plains reality. What did the 100th meridian mean for settlers?
West of the 100th meridian the climate is semi-arid, so 160 acres was too big to irrigate by hand but too small for dry farming.
6.1 Which pair of developments most directly ended the open range of the Cattle Kingdom?
Barbed wire let farmers fence claims and block cattle trails, while overgrazing and brutal winters wiped out herds on the open plains.
6.1 Why did many farmers join the Grange after the frontier closed?
Tied to global markets, farmers were at the mercy of railroads and grain storage fees, so the Grange fought for regulation.
6.2 General Sheridan called the buffalo the “commissary” of the Plains tribes. What did this show about U.S. strategy?
Total war meant destroying the resources that kept the tribes independent. Without buffalo, they faced surrender or starvation.
6.2 Why did the discovery of gold in the Black Hills lead to the Battle of Little Bighorn?
The treaty promised the hills to the Sioux, but miners poured in, the Sioux refused to sell, and the government set a deadline to label them hostile.
6.2 Figure 6.6 shows tribal land falling from 138 million acres in 1887 to 48 million in 1934. What caused this drop?
Allotment broke up shared tribal land, and any land left over was declared surplus and sold to white settlers.
6.2 How did the Carlisle Indian School carry out the policy of assimilation?
Students were put in uniforms, had their hair shorn, and were punished for speaking their languages or practicing their religions.
6.3 Why is the West described as a “borderland” rather than an empty frontier?
The text says the region was a meeting ground of many peoples, and a multiracial workforce built its economy.
6.3 What made Yick Wo v. Hopkins important for Chinese immigrants?
The Court held that the 14th Amendment’s Equal Protection Clause covers everyone in the country, regardless of citizenship.
6.3 How did the Land Act of 1851 hurt the Californios?
Families had to hire expensive lawyers in cases lasting decades, and about two-thirds of their land was lost.
6.3 Why have modern historians largely rejected Turner’s Frontier Thesis?
Turner left out the multiracial conflict of the West and treated Native Americans as part of the wilderness to be tamed.
