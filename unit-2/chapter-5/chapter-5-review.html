PART 3SECTION CHECKS · 1.1–1.4

Section Checkpoints

Choose the best answer. A short reason appears when you get one right.

5.1 Why did the Radical Republicans object to Lincoln’s 10% Plan?
The Radicals felt Lincoln’s “no-malice” peace was too soft on “traitors” and left out the needs of the freed slaves.
5.1 What was the major loophole in Johnson’s Presidential Reconstruction?
Johnson issued more than 13,000 pardons, so men like Alexander Stephens were elected to Southern governments and even to Congress.
5.1 What did the 1866 elections show about Johnson’s “Swing Around the Circle”?
Voters were repelled by his insults and shouting matches, so Republicans won two-thirds of both houses and could override any veto.
5.1 Why did the Senate fail to remove Johnson from office in 1868?
The vote was 36 to 19. Seven moderate Republicans voted “not guilty,” fearing that a conviction would damage the balance of power.
5.2 According to the chapter, what did most freedpeople believe true freedom required?
The text says freedom rested on three pillars: restoring families, creating independent institutions like churches and schools, and owning land.
5.2 Why did the Black church become the center of community life after emancipation?
The church was also a schoolhouse and a meeting place, so ministers often became political leaders.
5.2 What happened to the land that Sherman set aside for freedpeople in Special Field Orders No. 15?
Johnson ordered confiscated land returned, and the military evicted the nearly 40,000 freedpeople who had settled on “Sherman Land.”
5.2 Why did a sharecropper who worked hard all year often still end the year in debt?
Sharecroppers bought supplies on credit under the crop-lien system, with interest sometimes 50 or 60 percent, and merchants kept the ledger books.
5.3 What did the 14th Amendment do to the Supreme Court’s Dred Scott decision?
The first sentence of the amendment established birthright citizenship, erasing the ruling that Black Americans could never be citizens.
5.3 Why did Republicans decide to write the Civil Rights Act of 1866 into the Constitution?
An amendment places those principles beyond the reach of ordinary legislative majorities.
5.3 Why did the wording of the 15th Amendment leave a loophole for later Southern states?
Because it listed only race, color, and previous servitude, states used “color-blind” measures aimed at Black voters.
5.4 How did the White League differ from the Ku Klux Klan?
The Klan used hoods and secret night rides, while groups like the White League drilled in public and drove officials from office.
5.4 How did the Supreme Court’s ruling in United States v. Cruikshank weaken Reconstruction?
With the federal government unable to prosecute private groups, White League members felt free to intensify their intimidation.
5.4 What did Southern Democrats get in the Compromise of 1877?
Southern Democrats accepted Hayes as president in return for these conditions, and the last Republican governments soon collapsed.
