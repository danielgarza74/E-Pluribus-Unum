UNIT 2

Expansion and Exclusion

Chapter 5 opener photo. Image: Alfred R. Waud (Harper’s Weekly) / Wikimedia Commons, Public domain

Unit question

How did the United States rebuild itself after the Civil War, and why were so many groups systematically excluded from the promises of this new era?

California standards alignment (unit abstract): 11.1 (Constitution/Bill of Rights), 11.2 (Industrialization/Migration), 11.10 (Civil Rights)

Unit overview

After the Civil War, the United States had to decide what kind of nation it was rebuilding and who would belong to it. This unit argues that expansion and exclusion were not opposites. The same decades that remade the Constitution, joined the continent by rail and opened the West to settlement also drew lines around who could share in those gains.

Chapter 5 asks whether Reconstruction redefined American freedom or merely restored the old order. It follows the fight between the president and Congress over readmission, the ways freedpeople defined liberty through families, churches, schools and labor, the constitutional revolution of the 13th, 14th and 15th Amendments, and the collapse of Reconstruction. Chapter 6 turns west. It examines the railroad and the closing of the range, the wars for the Plains and the policies aimed at Native nations, and the diverse frontier where laws such as the Chinese Exclusion Act targeted particular groups.

Chapter 7 returns to the South, where the promise of the postwar amendments gave way to legal segregation and violence, and where African Americans developed new strategies to survive and resist. Students should come away able to explain how Reconstruction ended, how the nation's growth in the West depended on conquest and exclusion, and how Jim Crow was built and challenged, drawing on the chapters' primary sources.
