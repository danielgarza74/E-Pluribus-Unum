PART 3SECTION CHECKS · 1.1–1.4

Section Checkpoints

Choose the best answer. A short reason appears when you get one right.

17.1 According to George Kennan, why would a policy of containment be able to work against the Soviet Union?
Kennan said Stalin would probe for weaknesses but back down against strong resistance, and that a contained Soviet system would eventually collapse under its own flaws.
17.1 How did the Marshall Plan differ from the Truman Doctrine aid shown in Figure 17.3?
The Truman Doctrine sent $400 million to Greece and Turkey. The Marshall Plan sent about $13 billion to sixteen Western European countries to end the desperation that helped communist parties gain votes.
17.1 Why did Western European nations form NATO in April 1949?
The blockade convinced Western Europe it needed a formal guarantee of military defense. The Warsaw Pact came later, in 1955, as the Soviet response.
17.2 Why did Truman fire General MacArthur in April 1951?
MacArthur wanted to expand the war into China and wrote to newspapers and Republican politicians. Truman acted to preserve civilian control of the military.
17.2 What made peace under Mutually Assured Destruction a “terrifying paradox”?
Under MAD neither side dared strike first, so humanity’s survival rested on both sets of leaders being rational.
17.2 Why did the launch of Sputnik alarm Americans far more than its small size suggests?
An ICBM powerful enough to put a satellite in orbit could drop a hydrogen bomb on New York or Washington. The shock led to NASA and the National Defense Education Act.
17.3 How did the Hollywood Ten defend their refusal to answer HUAC’s questions?
They denounced the hearings as unconstitutional, but HUAC rejected the argument and they were convicted of contempt of Congress and imprisoned.
17.3 Why was Alger Hiss convicted of perjury instead of espionage?
Hiss was accused of spying in the 1930s. Because of the statute of limitations, a jury convicted him in 1950 of lying under oath to Congress.
17.3 What ended Senator McCarthy’s influence in 1954?
During the televised Army-McCarthy Hearings, Joseph Welch’s question “Have you no sense of decency, sir?” turned opinion against him, and the Senate censured him.
17.4 Why did the postwar economy boom instead of sliding back into depression?
Americans had saved about $135 billion during the war and rushed to buy goods once factories retooled. Defense spending and the Marshall Plan also kept growth steady.
17.4 How did William Levitt build homes so quickly and cheaply at Levittown?
Levitt standardized the design and pre-cut the lumber, so crews could build thousands of homes on a Long Island potato field. His early leases barred Black buyers.
17.4 How do revisionist historians read the data showing a boom in suburban homeownership?
Traditional historians see a “Golden Age” that democratized wealth, while revisionists point to restrictive covenants and redlining that locked minorities out of the main source of middle-class wealth.
17.5 What was the real effect of urban renewal on many minority neighborhoods?
Cleared land was rarely used for the promised affordable housing. It went to commercial buildings, stadiums, luxury apartments, and highways, and hundreds of thousands lost their homes.
17.5 Why did disc jockey Alan Freed popularize the term “rock ’n’ roll”?
Freed saw white teenagers buying rhythm and blues records in 1951 and played them on mainstream radio under a new name.
