PART 3SECTION CHECKS · 1.1–1.4

Section Checkpoints

Choose the best answer. A short reason appears when you get one right.

19.1 Why did Lyndon Johnson believe the federal government should use its wealth to help the poor?
Johnson grew up in rural poverty and taught poor children in a segregated Texas school. That experience left a lifelong belief that federal wealth should uplift the poorest citizens.
19.1 Which pair of Great Society laws aimed to improve health care, and who did each serve?
Figure 19.1 shows that Medicare covered older Americans while Medicaid extended insurance to welfare recipients and families below the poverty line.
19.1 How did the Tet Offensive of January 1968 hurt the Johnson administration even though it was a military defeat for communist forces?
Tet widened the Credibility Gap. After years of promises that the enemy was weak, television showed a country-wide assault, and even Walter Cronkite called the war a stalemate.
19.2 What most clearly caused the Generation Gap between baby boomers and their parents?
Parents prized security, hard work, and patriotism after the Depression and war. Their prosperous children, who practiced duck-and-cover drills, saw those same values as tools that stifled freedom.
19.2 According to the chapter, why did the Summer of Love of 1967 fall apart?
The sheer number of visitors strained the neighborhood. Runaways, overcrowding, and drugs such as methamphetamine and heroin ended the dream, and by autumn the founders held a mock funeral for the hippie.
19.2 What did the Altamont concert and the Manson crimes show about the end of the 1960s counterculture?
Woodstock had validated the hippie dream in August 1969. Four months later, violence at Altamont and the murders tied to Manson signaled the end of that dream.
19.3 What did Betty Friedan mean by “the problem that has no name” in The Feminine Mystique?
Friedan found that educated housewives felt empty even with the life society had promised them. She argued this was a systemic crisis built on a cultural myth, not an individual problem.
19.3 Why did Betty Friedan and others found NOW in 1966?
Title VII banned sex discrimination, but the male-dominated EEOC often refused to investigate. Frustrated, Friedan and about two dozen women created a group to force the law to be enforced.
19.3 According to Figure 19.8, how did radical feminists differ from NOW?
NOW worked through lawsuits and lobbying. Younger radicals, who had faced sexism inside the New Left, wanted to change society itself and believed that “the personal is political.”
19.3 Why did the Equal Rights Amendment fall three states short of ratification?
After 22 states ratified in the first year, Schlafly’s grassroots campaign stalled the ERA at 35 of the 38 states needed, and it died in 1982.
19.4 How did the UFW finally win contracts from California grape growers in 1970?
Farmworkers were left out of the 1935 labor law, so Chavez used the boycott. Millions stopped buying grapes, and the economic pressure broke the growers after a five-year fight.
19.4 Why did the American Indian Movement demand tribal sovereignty rather than integration?
Unlike the civil rights movement, which sought equal participation, Native activists demanded recognition as self-governing nations under the treaties, after Termination had harmed reservations and sent people to cities.
19.4 What did Stonewall, Alcatraz, and the East L.A. Blowouts have in common?
The chapter explains that the black freedom struggle served as a blueprint, teaching Latinos, Native Americans, and LGBTQ+ Americans the power of direct action and media attention.
