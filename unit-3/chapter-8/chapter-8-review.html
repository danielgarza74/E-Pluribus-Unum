PART 3SECTION CHECKS · 1.1–1.4

Section Checkpoints

Choose the best answer. A short reason appears when you get one right.

8.1 Why did the railroad companies create four standard time zones in 1883?
Different local times made scheduling a nightmare and caused head-on crashes on single-track lines, so the railroads fixed the problem themselves.
8.1 Why was the Central Pacific so dependent on Chinese immigrant workers?
The text says over 10,000 Chinese laborers were lowered in baskets down cliffs to set explosives, despite racism and lower wages than white workers.
8.1 How did the Bessemer process change American industry?
Blowing air through molten iron burned off carbon, making a day’s steel that once took a week and leading to steel rails, bridges, and skyscrapers.
8.1 Figure 8.3 shows 36,000 patents from 1790 to 1860 and 440,000 from 1860 to 1890. What does it suggest about the later period?
The first period was 70 years and the second only 30, so the contrast is even sharper. The patent system gave inventors a reason to invent.
8.2 How did Carnegie’s vertical integration help him beat his competitors?
By owning the mines, ships, and railroads, Carnegie cut out the middleman and could underbid rivals on every contract. Secret rebates were Rockefeller’s tool.
8.2 What was the main goal of Rockefeller’s horizontal integration?
Rockefeller bought or squeezed out rival refiners, and by 1880 Standard Oil controlled 90 percent of refining.
8.2 How did Social Darwinism help defend the great wealth of the Gilded Age?
Spencer and Sumner applied “survival of the fittest” to society, so success looked like proof of superior ability and regulation looked like harmful interference.
8.2 Why was the Sherman Antitrust Act weak in its first years?
In E.C. Knight (1895) the Court ruled that refining was “manufacturing,” not interstate commerce, and courts used the Act to break up unions.
8.3 Why did families send women and children to work in the late 1800s?
The family became a unit of survival that depended on several wages, so women and children were pulled into the workforce.
8.3 How did the Knights of Labor differ from the American Federation of Labor?
The Knights opened their doors to everyone “who toiled.” The AFL, led by Gompers, represented only skilled craft workers and focused on bread-and-butter issues.
8.3 How did the Haymarket Affair hurt the labor movement?
Newspapers fed a panic about “anarchists” and “socialists.” The Knights had nothing to do with the bomb, but their membership plummeted.
8.3 What do Homestead (1892) and Pullman (1894) show about how conflicts between workers and employers ended?
Pennsylvania’s governor sent 8,000 Guard troops at Homestead, and a court injunction and federal troops ended Pullman. Debs was arrested.
8.3 What was the main effect of the Triangle Shirtwaist Fire on American policy?
The fire killed 146 workers, many trapped behind locked doors. New York passed tough labor laws, and the fire marks the turn toward Progressive Era reform.
