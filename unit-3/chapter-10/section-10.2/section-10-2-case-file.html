1905A court ruling

Justice Rufus Peckham, U.S. Supreme Court

New York passed a law that limited bakers to 60 hours a week. A baker named Lochner said the state had no right to do this. The Supreme Court agreed with him.

“The statute necessarily interferes with the right of contract between the employer and employees, concerning the number of hours in which the latter may labor in the bakery of the employer. The general right to make a contract in relation to his business is part of the liberty of the individual protected by the 14th Amendment of the Federal Constitution. . . . The right to purchase or to sell labor is part of the liberty protected by this amendment, unless there are circumstances which exclude the right.”
Lochner v. New York, 198 U.S. 45 (1905), opinion of the Court by Justice Peckham; Cornell Legal Information Institute. Case citations left out are marked . . . .
Word help: statute = a law · interferes = gets in the way · contract = a signed agreement · employees = workers · liberty = freedom · protected = kept safe

1906A speech

President Theodore Roosevelt

Roosevelt used a story about a man with a muck-rake, a tool for raking filth. He was talking about writers who exposed corruption.

“Now, it is very necessary that we should not flinch from seeing what is vile and debasing. There is filth on the floor, and it must be scraped up with the muck-rake; and there are times and places where this service is the most needed of all the services that can be performed. But the man who never does anything else, who never thinks or speaks or writes save of his feats with the muckrake, speedily becomes, not a help to society, not an incitement to good, but one of the most potent forces for evil.”
Theodore Roosevelt, “The Man with the Muck-Rake,” remarks at the laying of the cornerstone of the House Office Building, April 14, 1906; The American Presidency Project, UC Santa Barbara.
Word help: flinch = pull back in fear · vile = very bad · debasing = lowering someone’s character · incitement = pushing someone to act · potent = strong · muck-rake = a tool for raking dirt

1908A court ruling

Justice David Brewer, U.S. Supreme Court

Oregon limited women laundry workers to ten hours a day. The lawyer Louis Brandeis argued for the law, and the Court upheld it.

“That woman’s physical structure and the performance of maternal functions place her at a disadvantage in the struggle for subsistence is obvious. This is especially true when the burdens of motherhood are upon her. Even when they are not, by abundant testimony of the medical fraternity, continuance for a long time on her feet at work, repeating this from day to day, tends to injurious effects upon the body, and, as healthy mothers are essential to vigorous offspring, the physical wellbeing of woman becomes an object of public interest and care in order to preserve the strength and vigor of the race.”
Muller v. Oregon, 208 U.S. 412 (1908), opinion of the Court by Justice Brewer; Justia U.S. Supreme Court Center.
Word help: maternal = having to do with being a mother · subsistence = earning enough to live · medical fraternity = doctors · injurious = harmful · offspring = children · vigor = strength

1911A speech

Rose Schneiderman, labor organizer

Schneiderman was a young garment worker and union leader. She spoke at a huge meeting about a week after the Triangle Shirtwaist fire.

“This is not the first time girls have been burned alive in the city. Every week I must learn of the untimely death of one of my sister workers. Every year thousands of us are maimed. The life of men and women is so cheap and property is so sacred. . . . I would be a traitor to these poor burned bodies if I came here to talk good fellowship.”
Rose Schneiderman, speech at the Metropolitan Opera House, New York, April 2, 1911, in Leon Stein, ed., Out of the Sweatshop (Quadrangle, 1977), 196–97; Cornell University, Kheel Center, Triangle Fire site. Words left out are marked . . . .
Word help: untimely = too early · maimed = badly hurt · sister workers = fellow women workers · sacred = holy, not to be touched · traitor = someone who betrays · good fellowship = friendly talk
