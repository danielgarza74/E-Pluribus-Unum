PART 3SECTION CHECKS · 1.1–1.4

Section Checkpoints

Choose the best answer. A short reason appears when you get one right.

10.1 Why did deflation after 1875 hurt farmers who had borrowed money?
Money gained value as greenbacks were pulled from circulation, so a farmer who borrowed when wheat sold for $2.00 faced the same debt with wheat under $1.00.
10.1 Figure 10.1 describes a “scissors effect” on farmers. What does that phrase mean?
Prices for crops fell while transportation and storage costs stayed high, which trapped families in a cycle of debt.
10.1 Why did the Populists demand the free coinage of silver?
Bimetallism would increase the money supply, causing inflation that raised crop prices and made debts easier to pay.
10.1 Why did Bryan lose the election of 1896 even though he thrilled farm audiences?
Bryan carried the South and mountain West and traveled 18,000 miles, but McKinley’s campaign warned city workers about food prices and won the Northeast, Midwest, and Pacific Coast.
10.2 Which result best shows the power of muckraking journalism?
Public disgust at Sinclair’s descriptions led Roosevelt to order an investigation and Congress to pass the Meat Inspection Act and the Pure Food and Drug Act.
10.2 What was one result of the Triangle Shirtwaist Factory fire of 1911?
After 146 workers died, a state commission investigated factories and its findings led to more than 50 laws.
10.2 Why does the chapter call Muller v. Oregon (1908) a complex victory for reformers?
Brandeis’s sociological brief won a ten-hour day for women, but arguing that women were “weaker” reinforced traditional gender roles.
10.2 What contradiction does the chapter point out in the Progressive view of democracy?
Many Progressives supported literacy tests and other restrictions aimed at immigrant voters even as they empowered “the people.”
10.3 How did Roosevelt’s handling of the 1902 coal strike differ from earlier presidents?
He threatened to seize the mines unless owners accepted a commission, and the miners won a 10 percent raise and a shorter workday.
10.3 Why did Progressive Republicans turn against President Taft?
Taft signed a tariff with only modest cuts and fired Gifford Pinchot, though he actually brought twice as many antitrust suits as Roosevelt.
10.3 Why did Woodrow Wilson win the election of 1912 with only 42 percent of the vote?
With Taft and Roosevelt dividing Republican voters, Wilson took a decisive Electoral College victory (435 votes).
10.3 How did Wilson’s New Freedom differ from Roosevelt’s New Nationalism?
Roosevelt’s New Nationalism would regulate large trusts, while Wilson believed trusts were dangerous and aimed for a “level playing field” for small businesses.
