1890A book

Jacob A. Riis, police reporter

Riis wrote about the tenements of New York. In this chapter he tells how the city tried to make landlords obey the law.

“. . . the adoption of the “Tenement-House Act” of 1867, the first step toward remedial legislation. . . . the Board ordered the cutting of more than forty-six thousand windows in interior rooms . . . owners of the tenements, who saw in every order to repair or clean up only an item of added expense . . . . Tenements are still “good property,” and the poverty of the poor man his destruction.”
Jacob A. Riis, How the Other Half Lives (1890), Chapter II, “The Awakening”; Standard Ebooks. Used here in short pieces.
Word help: adoption = passing a law · remedial = meant to fix a problem · legislation = laws · interior = inside · item of added expense = one more cost

1893A pamphlet

Ida B. Wells and her co-authors

The World’s Columbian Exposition in Chicago in 1893 was a huge fair with grand white buildings. Wells and her co-authors wrote this pamphlet about who was not included.

“The colored people of this great Republic number eight millions . . . . Why are they not taking part in this glorious celebration of the four-hundredth anniversary . . . . So when it is asked why we are excluded from the World’s Columbian Exposition, the answer is Slavery.”
Ida B. Wells, with Frederick Douglass, Irvine Garland Penn, and Ferdinand L. Barnett, The Reason Why the Colored American Is Not in the World’s Columbian Exposition (Chicago, 1893); University of Pennsylvania Digital Library, A Celebration of Women Writers. Used here in short pieces.
Word help: Republic = a country ruled by its citizens · celebration = a party for a special event · anniversary = a yearly date · excluded = kept out · Slavery = the system that held Black Americans as property

1896A magazine essay

Louis H. Sullivan, architect

Sullivan designed tall steel-frame buildings in Chicago. In this essay he explains what a tall building should look like.

“It demands of us, What is the chief characteristic of the tall office building? And at once we answer, it is lofty. . . . It must be tall, every inch of it tall. . . . It must be every inch a proud and soaring thing, rising in sheer exultation that from bottom to top it is a unit without a single dissenting line . . . .”
Louis H. Sullivan, “The Tall Office Building Artistically Considered,” Lippincott’s Magazine, March 1896; MIT OpenCourseWare, public domain. Used here in short pieces.
Word help: characteristic = main feature · lofty = very tall · exultation = joy and pride · unit = one whole piece · dissenting = disagreeing

1897A magazine article

George E. Waring Jr., New York’s street-cleaning chief

In 1895 Waring took charge of New York’s Department of Street Cleaning. Two years later he wrote about what had changed.

“Before 1895 the streets were almost universally in a filthy state. . . . The department was throttled by partisan control . . . . Their white uniforms, once so derided, have been a great help to them, and they know it . . . . What has really been done has been to put a man instead of a voter at the other end of the broom-handle.”
George E. Waring Jr., “The Cleaning of a Great City,” McClure’s Magazine, April 1897; reprinted online by The Brooklyn Rail, March 2012. Used here in short pieces.
Word help: universally = almost everywhere · throttled = choked off · partisan = tied to a political party · derided = laughed at · broom-handle = the stick of a broom
