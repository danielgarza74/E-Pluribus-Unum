PART 3SECTION CHECKS · 1.1–1.4

Section Checkpoints

Choose the best answer. A short reason appears when you get one right.

20.1 Why was the Saturday Night Massacre a turning point in the Watergate crisis?
Firing the man investigating him looked like a dictatorial abuse of power. The public outcry was so severe that the House began an inquiry into impeachment.
20.1 What did the Supreme Court decide in United States v. Nixon?
The Court ruled 8–0 that a limited privilege exists for military or diplomatic secrets but not for evidence in a criminal trial, so Nixon had to surrender the tapes.
20.1 How did the “Smoking Gun” tape help bring an end to Nixon's presidency?
The June 23, 1972 tape showed Nixon directing the cover-up from the start. Leaders told him he had fewer than fifteen Senate votes left, and he resigned on August 9, 1974.
20.2 Why was stagflation such a puzzle for policymakers in the 1970s?
The Keynesian seesaw had broken. Inflation and unemployment rose together, so a cure for one made the other worse.
20.2 What led the Arab members of OPEC to announce the 1973 oil embargo against the United States?
After the United States airlifted supplies to Israel during the Yom Kippur War, Arab OPEC members cut off oil to the United States and the world price of crude oil quadrupled.
20.2 According to the chapter, why did American steel mills close in the 1970s?
After World War II, American steel executives had little competition and stopped investing in new technology. By the 1970s, West Germany and Japan made better steel at a lower cost.
20.2 According to Figure 20.7, what helped the Sun Belt grow while the Rust Belt shrank?
The figure lists air conditioning, defense and aerospace spending, lower taxes, and right-to-work laws. Because House seats follow population, the move also shifted political power south and west.
20.3 Why did the Nixon Pardon cause such an angry public reaction?
Many Americans felt betrayed because the ordinary burglars were in prison while Nixon retired to California. Ford's approval ratings plummeted overnight.
20.3 Why did Ford's Whip Inflation Now campaign fail?
Asking struggling families to cut back looked out of touch, and voluntary action could not touch global oil prices. The campaign became a national punchline.
20.3 What did the Camp David Accords accomplish?
After thirteen days of talks led by Carter, Israel agreed to withdraw from the Sinai Peninsula, and Egypt guaranteed Israel's right to exist in peace.
20.3 What did the events at Love Canal lead to?
Toxic sludge in Niagara Falls homes forced Carter to declare a federal emergency, and the crisis led directly to Superfund. The nuclear halt followed Three Mile Island.
20.4 Why was All in the Family seen as a “cultural earthquake” for television?
Norman Lear's show used social realism to take on taboo subjects. It held the number-one spot on television for five years in a row.
20.4 According to Figure 20.13, how did punk rock differ from mainstream disco?
Punk's short, loud, three-chord songs and DIY spirit spoke for alienated working-class youth, while mainstream disco centered on personal pleasure and excess.
20.4 What did Title IX require of schools that received federal money?
Title IX of 1972 applied to schools receiving federal funds. The number of high school girls in organized sports rose from fewer than 300,000 in 1971 to over three million.
