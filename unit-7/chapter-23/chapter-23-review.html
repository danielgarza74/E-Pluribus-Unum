PART 3SECTION CHECKS · 1.1–1.4

Section Checkpoints

Choose the best answer. A short reason appears when you get one right.

23.1 Why did the Taliban become the first target of the War on Terror?
The Taliban sheltered bin Laden and al-Qaeda, ignored Bush’s ultimatum, and so the Afghanistan War began on October 7, 2001.
23.1 According to Figure 23.1, what stopped United Airlines Flight 93 from reaching Washington, D.C.?
Passengers who had learned of the other attacks fought the hijackers, and the plane crashed in an empty field in Shanksville, Pennsylvania.
23.1 Why did the Bush administration argue that the United States had to invade Iraq?
Officials warned that Hussein had biological and chemical agents and sought nuclear weapons. Later searches concluded he had no active WMD programs.
23.1 What did Edward Snowden’s 2013 leaks reveal to the American public?
Programs such as PRISM and the bulk collection of phone metadata showed a mass surveillance system built in secret after 9/11.
23.2 Why was the damage from Hurricane Katrina in New Orleans so severe?
Katrina had weakened to a Category 3, but the storm surge breached the Army Corps of Engineers’ levees, and 80 percent of the city flooded.
23.2 How did the Federal Reserve’s low interest rates help create the housing bubble?
The Fed cut rates after the dot-com bust and 9/11. Easy borrowing fueled speculation, and prices rose faster than the economy justified.
23.2 Why did many Americans become angry about the TARP bailout?
The sense of injustice fueled populist movements on both the left and the right, including the Tea Party.
23.2 What does Figure 23.8 show about the Great Recession?
The jobless rate more than doubled, which the chapter links to the freeze in credit after the housing collapse.
23.3 Why did the immigration debate become more militarized after September 11?
The new Department of Homeland Security absorbed immigration enforcement, creating CBP and ICE, and billions went to fencing, drones, and agents.
23.3 What did DACA do for the “Dreamers”?
DACA did not grant citizenship. Opponents sued, arguing that Obama had bypassed Congress.
23.3 Why did Congress repeal “Don’t Ask, Don’t Tell” in December 2010?
After years of campaigning, and with Obama’s support, the repeal let gay, lesbian, and bisexual service members serve openly.
23.4 How was Prestige TV different from older broadcast television?
Without sponsors or strict censorship, HBO could make shows like The Sopranos and The Wire with anti-heroes and long storylines.
23.4 How did social media change the way news and protests worked?
Citizen journalism helped movements like Black Lives Matter and the Arab Spring, but it also spread misinformation and created echo chambers.
23.4 According to the chapter, why were superhero movies so popular?
Audiences shaped by 9/11, the wars, and the Great Recession liked stories where heroes fixed what institutions could not.
