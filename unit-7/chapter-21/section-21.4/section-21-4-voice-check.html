César Chávez, leader of the United Farm Workers

Chávez led the United Farm Workers, a union for people who pick crops. He spoke in San Francisco in 1984.

“Today, thousands of farm workers live under savage conditions—beneath trees and amid garbage . . . near tomato fields in San Diego County—tomato fields which use the most modern farm technology. . . . They walk miles to buy food at inflated prices and they carry in water from irrigation pumps. Child labor is still common in many farm areas. As much as 30 percent of Northern California’s garlic harvesters are under-aged children.”
César Chávez, Address to the Commonwealth Club of California, San Francisco, November 9, 1984; California Department of Education, César Chávez Model Curriculum. Dashes shown as em dashes. A few words about living conditions were omitted with an ellipsis.
Word help: savage = harsh · technology = tools and machines · inflated prices = prices that are too high · irrigation pumps = machines that bring water to fields · harvesters = workers who pick crops
