PART 3SECTION CHECKS · 1.1–1.4

Section Checkpoints

Choose the best answer. A short reason appears when you get one right.

21.1 Why did Reagan’s question “Are you better off than you were four years ago?” work so well in 1980?
For millions of viewers dealing with high inflation, stagnant wages, and high interest rates, the answer was no, which made Reagan the clear alternative.
21.1 Which group of voters did the Reagan campaign pull away from the Democratic Party in 1980?
Reagan Democrats were mostly union workers who felt their party ignored their economic worries, and they gave Reagan the margin in key swing states.
21.1 Figure 21.1 lists the strands of the New Right. Which pairing is correct?
Falwell and Schlafly led the social conservative strand, while the Heritage Foundation represented the think-tank strand.
21.1 How did Sandra Day O’Connor’s time on the Supreme Court differ from what the New Right hoped for?
The New Right wanted a hardline conservative, but O’Connor proved pragmatic and was often the swing vote on abortion and affirmative action.
21.2 According to supply-side theory, what should happen when taxes on the wealthy and corporations are cut?
Supply-side theory said that producers and investors would put their savings into new technology and factories rather than hoarding it.
21.2 Why did the national debt explode during the Reagan years?
The claim that tax cuts would pay for themselves proved false, and with trillions going to the military, spending far outpaced revenue.
21.2 Why was the federal response to the AIDS crisis so slow?
The earliest victims were mostly gay men and drug users, and Reagan, who relied on the Moral Majority, did not say the word “AIDS” in public until 1985.
21.3 What was the main purpose of the Reagan military buildup and SDI?
Peace through Strength aimed to ensure American supremacy and to push the struggling Soviet economy toward reform and arms treaties.
21.3 Why was the Iran-Contra Affair called a constitutional crisis?
Staffers sold missiles to Iran and diverted profits to the Contras despite the Boland Amendment, raising questions about executive power and democratic oversight.
21.3 What was the effect of Gorbachev’s decision not to send tanks against the 1989 uprisings in Eastern Europe?
Earlier Soviet leaders had used tanks, but Gorbachev held back, and a wave of peaceful revolutions followed, leading to German reunification in October 1990.
21.3 What did the Powell Doctrine say about the use of military force?
Colin Powell, shaped by Vietnam, wanted to avoid vague, incremental wars, which guided the swift one-hundred-hour ground war against Iraq.
21.4 How did CNN change the way American leaders had to deal with news?
The 24-hour news cycle meant that the public watched events in real time, which became known as the “CNN Effect.”
21.4 Figure 21.14 shows that Michael Jackson, Madonna, and Run-D.M.C. each used MTV to do what?
Jackson broke MTV’s unofficial color line, Madonna took control of her image, and Run-D.M.C. brought hip hop to suburban America.
21.4 What did the 1987 film Wall Street and Tom Wolfe’s novel Bonfire of the Vanities have in common?
The film captured and critiqued the decade’s greed (“Greed… is good”), and Wolfe’s novel about a fallen bond trader exposed the gap between the wealthy elite and the struggling poor.

“In this present crisis, government is not the solution to our problem; government is the problem. From time to time we’ve been tempted to believe that society has become too complex to be managed by self-rule, that government by an elite group is superior to government for, by, and of the people. Well, if no one among us is capable of governing himself, then who among us has the capacity to govern someone else?”
President Ronald Reagan, First Inaugural Address, January 20, 1981.

Ryan White, testimony before the President’s Commission on the HIV Epidemic, March 3, 1988.
