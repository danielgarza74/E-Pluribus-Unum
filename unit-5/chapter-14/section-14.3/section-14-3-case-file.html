1933A message to Congress

President Franklin D. Roosevelt

Roosevelt had been President for less than three weeks. He asked Congress for a new kind of jobs program.

“I propose to create a civilian conservation corps to be used in simple work, not interfering with normal employment, and confining itself to forestry, the prevention of soil erosion, flood control and similar projects. I call your attention to the fact that this type of work is of definite, practical value, not only through the prevention of great present financial loss, but also as a means of creating future national wealth.”
Franklin D. Roosevelt, Message to Congress on Unemployment Relief, March 21, 1933; The American Presidency Project, UC Santa Barbara. The source prints “civilian conservation corps” in lowercase.
Word help: corps = a group of workers organized together · interfering = getting in the way · confining = limiting · forestry = caring for forests · erosion = wearing away of soil · definite = clear · national wealth = the country’s riches

1933A signing statement

President Franklin D. Roosevelt

Roosevelt signed a law that created the National Recovery Administration, or NRA. He told reporters what it was meant to do.

“History probably will record the National Industrial Recovery Act as the most important and far-reaching legislation ever enacted by the American Congress. Its goal is the assurance of a reasonable profit to industry and living wages for labor with the elimination of the piratical methods and practices which have not only harassed honest business but also contributed to the ills of labor. . . . Obviously, if this project is to succeed, it demands the wholehearted cooperation of industry, labor and every citizen of the Nation.”
Franklin D. Roosevelt, Statement on Signing the National Industrial Recovery Act, June 16, 1933; The American Presidency Project, UC Santa Barbara. One middle passage left out.
Word help: enacted = passed into law · far-reaching = with big effects · assurance = a promise · piratical = like a pirate, greedy and unfair · harassed = bothered again and again · wholehearted = fully committed

1935A court opinion

U.S. Supreme Court, Chief Justice Hughes

A poultry company in New York was accused of breaking the NRA’s codes. The Supreme Court ruled against the government, 9 to 0.

“Undoubtedly, the conditions to which power is addressed are always to be considered when the exercise of power is challenged. Extraordinary conditions may call for extraordinary remedies. But the argument necessarily stops short of an attempt to justify action which lies outside the sphere of constitutional authority. Extraordinary conditions do not create or enlarge constitutional power.”
A.L.A. Schechter Poultry Corp. v. United States, 295 U.S. 495 (1935), opinion of the Court by Chief Justice Charles Evans Hughes, decided May 27, 1935; Justia U.S. Supreme Court Center.
Word help: undoubtedly = without doubt · addressed = aimed at · remedies = fixes · justify = give a good reason for · sphere = area · constitutional authority = power the Constitution gives · enlarge = make bigger

1936A dinner speech

Alfred E. Smith, former governor of New York

Smith had been the Democratic candidate for President in 1928. Here he spoke to the American Liberty League, a group that opposed the New Deal.

“There can be only one Capitol – Washington or Moscow. There can be only one atmosphere of government, the clear, pure, fresh air of free America, or the foul breath of Communistic Russia. There can be only one flag, the Stars and Stripes, or the Red Flag of the godless union of the Soviet.”
Alfred E. Smith, “The Facts in the Case,” American Liberty League dinner, Washington, D.C., January 25, 1936; Teaching American History (Ashbrook Center), from the Jouett Shouse Collection, University of Kentucky Libraries.
Word help: atmosphere = the feeling or setting of a place · foul = rotten · Communistic = having to do with communism, a system where the government controls the economy · Moscow = the capital of the Soviet Union · godless = without religion
