PART 3SECTION CHECKS · 1.1–1.4

Section Checkpoints

Choose the best answer. A short reason appears when you get one right.

14.1 Why did the text say the 1920s boom rested on “thin ice” even though factories were producing more than ever?
The wealthy gained most of the income while ordinary workers could not afford what factories made, creating underconsumption and overflowing warehouses.
14.1 Why did buying on margin make the stock market so dangerous when prices began to fall?
Margin calls forced investors with no cash reserves to sell, which drove prices lower and triggered still more margin calls.
14.1 According to the chapter, how did the Hawley-Smoot Tariff affect the economy?
The tariff was meant to protect American jobs but backfired as other countries struck back, turning a national recession into a worldwide depression.
14.1 Figure 14.3 shows unemployment reaching 25 percent nationally in 1933 and over 50 percent for African Americans in some cities in 1932. What does the comparison show?
The text explains that discrimination meant “last hired, first fired,” so the rate for Black workers in some cities was double the national average.
14.2 How did Hoover’s philosophy of rugged individualism shape his response to the Depression?
Hoover feared that direct relief would destroy initiative and create dependence, so he urged business and charity to help voluntarily.
14.2 What did the Election of 1932 show about American politics?
Roosevelt won about 57 percent of the vote and 472 electoral votes to Hoover’s 59, a realignment that gave Democrats a dominant place for decades.
14.2 Why did Roosevelt close every bank before reopening any of them?
The Bank Holiday and the Emergency Banking Relief Act replaced panic with orderly federal supervision, and when banks reopened people made deposits.
14.3 The chapter sorts New Deal programs into Relief, Recovery, and Reform. Which program belongs under Reform?
Reform programs were meant to be permanent fixes to the financial system, such as the FDIC and the SEC, while relief and recovery were more temporary.
14.3 Why were some Americans outraged by the AAA?
People in breadlines were angered by the plowing under of cotton and the slaughter of pigs, and landowners often evicted tenant farmers who were no longer needed.
14.3 What was the Supreme Court’s ruling in Schechter Poultry Corp. v. U.S. (1935)?
The justices ruled unanimously that the law gave the president too much legislative power and reached a business that operated only locally.
14.3 Why did Roosevelt “lose the battle but win the war” over the Court Packing Plan?
Critics called the plan an attack on judicial independence and it failed, yet one justice changed his votes and another retired, which became the “switch in time that saved nine.”
14.4 Why did Roosevelt fund Social Security through payroll contributions from workers and employers?
Roosevelt said the payroll taxes gave contributors a legal, moral, and political right to benefits, so no politician could easily abolish them.
14.4 How did the Wagner Act change the balance of power in American workplaces?
Employers could no longer fire or threaten workers for organizing, and if a majority voted for a union the employer had to bargain in good faith.
14.4 The Indian Reorganization Act of 1934 reversed the policy of the Dawes Act of 1887. What was the key change?
The act ended allotment, returned surplus lands to tribes, and let tribes set up their own governments and businesses under Commissioner John Collier.
