1934A law

Congress and President Roosevelt

For almost fifty years, the Dawes Act had broken up tribal land into small plots. Congress passed this law in 1934 to stop that.

“On and after June 18, 1934, no land of any Indian reservation, created or set apart by treaty or agreement with the Indians, Act of Congress, Executive order, purchase, or otherwise, shall be allotted in severalty to any Indian. . . . The existing periods of trust placed upon any Indian lands and any restriction on alienation thereof are extended and continued until otherwise directed by Congress.”
Indian Reorganization Act (Wheeler-Howard Act), June 18, 1934, ch. 576, §§ 1–2, 48 Stat. 984, as codified at 25 U.S.C. §§ 5101–5102; Legal Information Institute, Cornell Law School. The codified wording may differ slightly from the 1934 text. Two sections joined with an omission mark.
Word help: reservation = land set aside for a Native nation · allotted = divided up and handed out · in severalty = to one person alone, not shared · alienation = giving up or selling land · trust = land that the government holds and protects · directed = ordered

1935A law

Congress and President Roosevelt

Congress passed this law to protect workers who wanted to join unions. It created the National Labor Relations Board.

“The inequality of bargaining power between employees who do not possess full freedom of association or actual liberty of contract, and employers who are organized in the corporate or other forms of ownership association substantially burdens and affects the flow of commerce . . . . Employees shall have the right of self-organization, to form, join, or assist labor organizations, to bargain collectively through representatives of their own choosing, and to engage in concerted activities, for the purpose of collective bargaining or other mutual aid or protection.”
National Labor Relations Act (Wagner Act), July 5, 1935, Sections 1 and 7; Pepperdine University School of Public Policy, New Deal Legislation. Most of Section 1 left out.
Word help: bargaining power = ability to get a fair deal · freedom of association = freedom to join groups · liberty of contract = freedom to make a job deal on your own terms · burdens = weighs down · collectively = together as a group · concerted = done together · mutual aid = helping each other

1936A campaign speech

Alf Landon, Republican candidate for President

Landon ran against Roosevelt in 1936. He spoke about the new payroll taxes that paid for the Social Security Act.

“Beginning next January employers must, in addition, begin paying taxes on the payrolls out of which your wages are to come. This is the largest tax bill in history. And to call it ‘social security’ is a fraud on the workingman. These taxes start at the rate of $2 in taxes for every $100 of wages. They increase until it is $6 in taxes for every $100 in wages.”
Alf Landon, “I Will Not Promise the Moon,” Vital Speeches of the Day, October 15, 1936, pp. 26–27; History Matters, George Mason University. Quotation marks inside the quote changed to single marks.
Word help: payrolls = the money a company pays its workers · fraud = a trick to cheat people · workingman = a worker · rate = the amount charged

1936A campaign speech

President Franklin D. Roosevelt

Three days before the 1936 election, Roosevelt spoke to a huge crowd in New York City. He answered his critics, including those who attacked Social Security.

“We know now that Government by organized money is just as dangerous as Government by organized mob. Never before in all our history have these forces been so united against one candidate as they stand today. They are unanimous in their hate for me—and I welcome their hatred. I should like to have it said of my first Administration that in it the forces of selfishness and of lust for power met their match.”
Franklin D. Roosevelt, Address at Madison Square Garden, New York City, October 31, 1936; The American Presidency Project, UC Santa Barbara.
Word help: organized money = rich people and big business working together · organized mob = an angry crowd · unanimous = all agreeing · selfishness = caring only about yourself · lust for power = a hunger to rule · met their match = found an equal
