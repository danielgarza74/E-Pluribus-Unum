PART 3SECTION CHECKS · 1.1–1.4

Section Checkpoints

Choose the best answer. A short reason appears when you get one right.

16.1 According to the chapter, what did the huge growth in wartime government spending do to the Great Depression?
Billions in military contracts reopened factories. Unemployment fell from 14.6 percent in 1940 to about 1 percent in 1944.
16.1 Figure 16.1 shows Liberty Ship build times falling from about a year to 45 days and then to 4 days. What does the chart best show?
Henry J. Kaiser applied mass-production principles to standardized ships. The “miracle of production” was about how factories worked, not only what they made.
16.1 Why did the Office of Price Administration freeze prices and issue ration books?
With steady wages and fewer goods, prices threatened to soar. Rationing made “fair share” the law, and a purchase without a coupon was illegal.
16.2 Why did the government describe women’s war work as being “for the duration” of the war?
Women filled welding, machinist, and shipfitter jobs, but propaganda told them to give up tools and uniforms when the “boys” came home. That set up a postwar cultural clash.
16.2 A. Philip Randolph threatened a march of 100,000 African Americans on Washington. What did Roosevelt do in response?
Order 8802 banned discrimination in defense industries and government. It was the first federal action protecting Black economic rights since Reconstruction.
16.2 Figure 16.5 compares opportunities and obstacles for three groups. What do all three rows have in common?
Women, African Americans, and Mexican Americans all found new work but met lower pay, segregation, harsh conditions, or violence.
16.2 What did the Detroit Race Riot of June 1943 reveal, according to the chapter?
New arrivals overwhelmed Detroit’s housing, and rumors set off three days of violence. The mostly white police were accused of joining in, so federal troops had to restore order.
16.3 Why does the chapter say Executive Order 9066 revealed that “military necessity” was really racial animosity?
The FBI and Office of Naval Intelligence found zero evidence of a conspiracy, yet only Japanese Americans were removed, about 120,000 people, many of them citizens.
16.3 What did the Nisei soldiers of the 442nd Regimental Combat Team show through their service, even as their families stayed in camps?
The 442nd became the most decorated unit for its size and service, and its record was a rebuke to the “Fifth Column” hysteria.
16.3 Justice Jackson called the Korematsu ruling a “loaded weapon.” What danger was he describing?
Jackson said a court opinion stays in the law long after a military order ends, so it lies ready for any authority that claims an urgent need.
16.4 How was the United Nations designed to fix weaknesses of the League of Nations?
The League lacked both American participation and the power to enforce its decisions. The Security Council became the UN’s “teeth.”
16.4 Under the Bretton Woods System, why did the United States gain great influence over the world economy?
The dollar became the main reserve currency, and the U.S. supplied most of the funding for the IMF and World Bank.
16.4 What principle did the Nuremberg Trials establish about people who commit atrocities?
For the first time, high-ranking officials were held criminally responsible. Nuremberg rejected the defense of “following orders” and created the category of Crimes Against Humanity.
16.4 The chapter says the G.I. Bill helped build a vast middle class. Why did it also widen the racial wealth gap?
The law was race-neutral on paper, but local administration was often discriminatory, so Black veterans were shut out of the new “whites-only” suburbs and some colleges.
