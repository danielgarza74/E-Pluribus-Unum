PART 3SECTION CHECKS · 1.1–1.4

Section Checkpoints

Choose the best answer. A short reason appears when you get one right.

15.1 Why did many people in struggling nations turn to “strongman” leaders in the 1930s?
The Great Depression destroyed faith in democratic governments, so desperate people looked to leaders who promised national pride, order, and economic recovery.
15.1 How did the League of Nations respond to Italy’s invasion of Ethiopia in 1935, and what did that show?
The League refused to ban oil sales or close the Suez Canal to Italy, so the sanctions were useless and Mussolini’s victory showed a strong nation could ignore international law.
15.1 What did Hitler do after the Munich Pact that proved appeasement had failed?
Hitler promised he would seek no more territory, but only six months later he took the rest of Czechoslovakia, as Churchill had warned.
15.2 Why did Roosevelt’s 1937 Quarantine Speech fail to change American policy?
Chicago was a stronghold of isolationism and the backlash was fierce. Roosevelt realized he could not lead the nation into cooperation until public opinion changed.
15.2 Roosevelt compared aid to Britain to lending a neighbor a garden hose. What was the point of that analogy?
The simple image shifted the focus from the price of the aid to the shared danger of the fire spreading to your own house.
15.2 Why did the American oil embargo of July 1941 push Japan toward war?
With only a few months of oil reserves, Japan’s planners chose to seize the oil-rich Dutch East Indies, knowing it meant war with the United States.
15.3 Why did Stalin demand a “Second Front” in Western Europe?
Soviet armies were taking the full weight of the German Wehrmacht, so Stalin wanted a new front that would divide Germany’s armies.
15.3 What finally turned the tide of the Allied air war over Germany in 1944?
Until 1944 bombers lacked fighter escorts for the whole trip. The P-51 protected them and destroyed the German air force, giving the Allies air superiority.
15.3 How did Operation Fortitude help the D-Day landings succeed?
The “phantom army” of inflatable tanks and fake radio signals convinced Hitler the invasion would land at Pas-de-Calais, even after it began at Normandy.
15.4 Why was the Battle of Midway the turning point of the Pacific war?
Because American codebreakers had cracked Japan’s naval codes, the carriers ambushed the Japanese fleet. Losing four carriers ended Japanese expansion.
15.4 Why did Truman’s advisers reject a demonstration of the atomic bomb on an empty island?
According to the chapter, a demonstration might fail to impress hardliners, so only the overwhelming shock of a bomb on a city was expected to force immediate surrender.
15.5 What was the main effect of the Nuremberg Laws of 1935?
The Reich Citizenship Law and the Law for the Protection of German Blood and German Honor made racial discrimination official, counting anyone with three or four Jewish grandparents as Jewish.
15.5 How were Death Camps such as Treblinka different from Concentration Camps?
Concentration camps were used for detention and slave labor. Death camps were “murder factories” made for mass execution upon arrival.
15.5 What revolutionary principle did the Nuremberg War Crimes Trials establish?
The trials rejected the “superior orders” defense and created the new legal concept of crimes against humanity, a base for modern international law.
