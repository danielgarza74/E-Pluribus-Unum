SOURCE A1920 · A declaration

Marcus Garvey, Declaration of Rights

Garvey’s UNIA held a huge convention in New York in August 1920. Delegates approved this declaration.

“[B]e it known to all men that whereas all men are created equal and entitled to the rights of life, liberty and the pursuit of happiness, and because of this we, the duly elected representatives of the Negro peoples of the world, invoking the aid of the just and Almighty God, do declare all men, women and children of our blood throughout the world free denizens, and do claim them as free citizens of Africa, the Motherland of all Negroes.”
Universal Negro Improvement Association, “Declaration of Rights of the Negro Peoples of the World,” New York, August 1920, first article; BlackPast (blackpast.org). The first words are shortened to “[B]e it known.”
Word help: whereas = since · endowed = given · invoking = calling on · denizens = people who live in a place · Motherland = homeland

SOURCE B1920s · A poem

Langston Hughes, “I, Too”

Hughes wrote this short poem in the mid-1920s, during the Harlem Renaissance.

“I, too, sing America. / I am the darker brother. / They send me to eat in the kitchen / When company comes, / But I laugh, / And eat well, / And grow strong. / Tomorrow, / I’ll be at the table / When company comes. / Nobody’ll dare / Say to me, / “Eat in the kitchen,” / Then. / Besides, / They’ll see how beautiful I am / And be ashamed— / I, too, am America.”
Langston Hughes, “I, Too,” mid-1920s; Poetry Foundation (poetryfoundation.org). Line breaks shown with slashes.
Word help: darker brother = a Black American · company = guests · dare = be brave enough · beautiful = lovely · ashamed = feeling sorry about something wrong
