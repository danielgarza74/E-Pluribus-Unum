1925A trial statement

The defense team in the Scopes Trial

John Scopes, a Tennessee teacher, was on trial for teaching evolution. His lawyers made this statement of their case.

“The defense maintains that there is no more justification for imposing the conflicting views of the Bible on courses of biology than there would be for imposing the views of biologists on courses of comparative religion. We maintain that science and religion embrace two separate and distinct fields of thought and learning. . . . The narrow purpose of the defense is to establish the innocence of the defendant Scopes. The broad purpose of the defense will be to prove that the Bible is a work of religious aspiration and rules of conduct which must be kept in the field of theology.”
“Summary of the Defense,” Tennessee v. Scopes, July 1925, Hanover Historical Texts Project (history.hanover.edu). One typo fixed (“biologists”).
Word help: justification = a good reason · imposing = forcing on · embrace = include · aspiration = high hope · theology = the study of religion

1925A speech

William Jennings Bryan, lawyer for the prosecution

Bryan wrote this speech to give at the end of the Scopes Trial. The trial ended before he could give it.

“Science is a magnificent force, but it is not a teacher of morals. It can perfect machinery, but it adds no moral restraints to protect society from the misuse of the machine. It can also build gigantic intellectual ships, but it constructs no moral rudders for the control of storm tossed human vessel. It not only fails to supply the spiritual element needed but some of its unproven hypotheses rob the ship of its compass and thus endangers its cargo.”
William Jennings Bryan, prepared closing argument, Tennessee v. Scopes, July 1925 (never delivered in court); Famous Trials, via Hanover Historical Texts Project (history.hanover.edu).
Word help: magnificent = great · restraints = limits · misuse = using wrongly · intellectual = of the mind · hypotheses = guesses · rudders = steering tools · cargo = what a ship carries

1926A magazine article

Hiram W. Evans, Imperial Wizard of the Ku Klux Klan

Evans led the Ku Klux Klan. He wrote this article for a national magazine to explain what the Klan stood for.

“There are three . . . great racial instincts, vital elements in both the historic and the present attempts to build an America which shall fulfill the aspirations and justify the heroism of the men who made the nation. These are the instincts of loyalty to the white race, to the traditions of America, and to the spirit of Protestantism . . . . They are condensed into the Klan slogan: “Native, white, Protestant supremacy.” First in the Klansman’s mind is patriotism-America for Americans.”
Hiram W. Evans, “The Klan’s Fight for Americanism,” North American Review, 1926; The American Yawp (americanyawp.com).
Word help: instincts = natural urges · aspirations = hopes · heroism = great bravery · supremacy = rule over others · patriotism = love of country

1927A courtroom statement

Bartolomeo Vanzetti, Italian immigrant and anarchist

Vanzetti was a fish peddler. He and Nicola Sacco were found guilty of murder. He spoke before the judge sentenced them to death.

“If it had not been for these thing I might have lived out my life talking at street corners to scorning men. I might have die, unmarked, unknown, a failure. Now we are not a failure. This is our career and our triumph. Never in our full life could we hope to do such work for tolerance, for justice, for men's understanding of man, as now we do by accident. Our words, our lives, our pains - nothing! The taking of our lives - lives of a good shoemaker and a poor fishpeddler - all!”
Bartolomeo Vanzetti, last statement to the court, 1927 (commonly dated April 9, 1927); Pitzer College Anarchy Archives. Vanzetti’s own English and spelling are kept.
Word help: scorning = looking down on · unmarked = not remembered · tolerance = accepting people who are different · accident = by chance · peddler = someone who sells goods on the street
