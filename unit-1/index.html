UNIT 1

The Forging of the Union

Chapter 1 opener photo. Photo: Mys_721tx / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0

Unit question

How did a collection of diverse colonies become a single nation, and why did that nation eventually tear itself apart?

California standards alignment (unit abstract): 11.1 (Founding documents/principles), 11.3 (Religion and philosophy), 11.10 (Civil Rights/Constitution context)

Unit overview

This unit follows one question through the founding era and the first half of the nineteenth century: could a collection of diverse colonies govern itself as a single nation? Chapter 1 traces the break from British authority and the two experiments that followed, the Articles of Confederation and the Constitution. It shows that the founders built a tension between federal authority and local liberty into the republic from the start, and that the Constitution's compromises over slavery were part of that design.

Chapters 2 and 3 test that design. The early republic fought over parties, the Louisiana Purchase, the Nullification crisis and Indian Removal while Jacksonian democracy widened political participation and displaced Native nations. Meanwhile, the North and South grew apart. Markets and reform shaped the North, cotton and slavery shaped the South, and western expansion forced the question of slavery into national politics through the Mexican-American War, the Dred Scott decision and the election of Lincoln.

Chapter 4 treats the Civil War as both the failure of the original system and the making of a new one. It follows the strategies of the two sides, the shift in war aims toward emancipation, the turning points at Antietam, Gettysburg and Vicksburg, and the human cost of the fighting. After this unit, students should be able to explain how the founding bargains produced both a union and the conditions for its breakup, using evidence from the chapters' sources to support the argument.
