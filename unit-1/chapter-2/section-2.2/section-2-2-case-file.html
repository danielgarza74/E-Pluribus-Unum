1803A private letter

President Thomas Jefferson

Jefferson wrote to a senator weeks after hearing about the Louisiana deal. He had always said the Constitution lists the government’s powers.

“The Constitution has made no provision for our holding foreign territory, still less for incorporating foreign nations into our Union. The Executive in seizing the fugitive occurrence which so much advances the good of their country, have done an act beyond the Constitution. The Legislature in casting behind them metaphysical subtleties, and risking themselves like faithful servants, must ratify & pay for it, and throw themselves on their country for doing for them unauthorized what we know they would have done for themselves had they been in a situation to do it.”
Thomas Jefferson to John Breckinridge, August 12, 1803; Teaching American History (from Founders Online). Original spelling and “&” kept.
Word help: provision = a rule that allows something · foreign territory = land owned by another country · incorporating = bringing in · Executive = the President’s branch · metaphysical subtleties = fine points of argument · ratify = approve

1812A war message

President James Madison

Madison asked Congress to decide on war with Britain. He listed British actions against American ships and sailors.

“British cruisers have been in the continued practice of violating the American flag on the great highway of nations, and of seizing and carrying off persons sailing under it, not in the exercise of a belligerent right founded on the law of nations against an enemy, but of a municipal prerogative over British subjects. . . . a self-redress is assumed which, if British subjects were wrongfully detained and alone concerned, is that substitution of force for a resort to the responsible sovereign which falls within the definition of war.”
James Madison, War Message to Congress, June 1, 1812 (shortened); Teaching American History (from Founders Online).
Word help: cruisers = fast warships · belligerent right = a right of a nation at war · law of nations = rules between countries · prerogative = special power · self-redress = fixing a wrong yourself · sovereign = ruler

1813A newspaper letter

James Forten, a free Black Philadelphian

Forten was a free Black sailmaker. Pennsylvania lawmakers were considering a bill to limit free Black people. He published his answer without his name.

“We hold this truth to be self-evident, that God created all men equal, and is one of the most prominent features in the Declaration of Independence, and in that glorious fabrick of collected wisdom, our noble Constitution. This idea embraces the Indian and the European, the Savage and the Saint, the Peruvian and the Laplander, the white Man and the African, and whatever measures are adopted subversive of this inestimable privilege, are in direct violation of the letter and spirit of our Constitution . . . .”
James Forten (as “A Man of Colour”), Letters from a Man of Colour, Letter I, April 1813 (shortened); Libertarianism.org text, based on the Gilder Lehrman Institute’s copy. Spelling kept; the site hyphenates “self-evident.”
Word help: prominent = stands out · fabrick = fabric, here the structure of the Constitution · embraces = includes · subversive = working to destroy · inestimable = too valuable to measure · privilege = right

1814–1815A report

The Hartford Convention

New England Federalists met in Connecticut during the war. They did not like “Mr. Madison’s War.”

“The mode and the energy of the opposition, should always conform to the nature of the violation, the intention of its authors, the extent of the injury inflicted, the determination manifested to persist in it, and the danger of delay. But in cases of deliberate, dangerous, and palpable infractions of the Constitution, affecting the sovereignty of a State, and liberties of the people; it is not only the right but the duty of such a State to interpose its authority for their protection, in the manner best calculated to secure that end.”
Report of the Hartford Convention, January 1815 (shortened); Teaching American History (from Public Documents, Containing Proceedings of the Hartford Convention, Massachusetts Senate, 1815).
Word help: mode = way · energy = force · infractions = violations · palpable = obvious · sovereignty = supreme power · interpose = step in · authority = power
