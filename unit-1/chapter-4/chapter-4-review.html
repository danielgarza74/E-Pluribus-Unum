PART 3SECTION CHECKS · 1.1–1.4

Section Checkpoints

Choose the best answer. A short reason appears when you get one right.

4.1 Why did the North’s industrial mobilization give it such an edge in a long war?
The North held more than 90 percent of manufacturing and over 20,000 miles of mostly standard-gauge railroad, so it could supply and move huge armies.
4.1 What did the Confederate draft law of 1862 and the Union Enrollment Act of 1863 have in common?
Slaveholders with twenty or more enslaved people could avoid the Confederate draft, and Northern men could pay $300 or hire a substitute. Both produced class resentment.
4.1 Why did Sherman bring the “hard hand of war” to Georgia’s civilians on his March to the Sea?
Sherman’s troops lived off the land and destroyed anything of military or economic value, shattering the Southern economy and the will to fight.
4.2 Why did Lincoln move slowly against slavery in the early months of the war?
Lincoln remarked that he “must have Kentucky.” If the Border States left the Union, the North could lose key rivers, resources, and even Washington, D.C.
4.2 What was the importance of the contraband policy at Fort Monroe in May 1861?
General Butler kept fugitives as seized property of war. Enslaved people crossing Union lines by the thousands pushed the government toward emancipation.
4.2 Why did Lincoln wait for a Union victory before announcing the Emancipation Proclamation?
After the victory at Antietam, Lincoln issued the preliminary proclamation, which became final on January 1, 1863.
4.3 Why is the Battle of Antietam called a strategic Union victory even though the fighting was a tactical draw?
One-third of Lee’s men were killed or wounded, so he could not continue the campaign. Lincoln then issued the preliminary proclamation.
4.3 How did the Union victories of early July 1863 change the war?
Lee never again had the strength for a major offensive, and the Union controlled the whole Mississippi, cutting off Texas, Arkansas, and Louisiana.
4.3 What does Grant’s strategy in 1864 show about the North’s advantages?
Grant understood that even if he lost two men for every one of Lee’s, the North would win the war of numbers. He lost 50,000 men in six weeks and kept moving south.
4.4 According to the chapter, what was the main cause of death for soldiers in the Civil War?
Neither army understood germ theory. Crowded camps and unsanitary latrines spread measles, dysentery, and typhoid fever.
4.4 Why did the Civil War destroy the ideal of the Good Death for so many families?
Of roughly 750,000 dead, nearly half were listed as “unknown,” which left families in a state of “ambiguous loss.”
4.4 What does the chapter say happened at Andersonville and Elmira?
Nearly 13,000 Union prisoners died at Andersonville and nearly 3,000 of 12,000 Confederate prisoners died at Elmira, which the text calls the darkest depths of total war.
4.4 Why does the chapter call the Civil War pension system a fundamental shift in the role of the federal government?
The pension system was the nation’s first major social welfare program, helping widows, orphans, and disabled veterans and laying the groundwork for the modern safety net.
